Terms of Service

A free tool from a university project, offered as is. It will not make your deck legally compliant on its own, a person still has to check the result, and there are a few things you shouldn't send it.

Last updated Nov 20, 2026

What this covers

These terms apply to this website and the tool at accessibilityenhancer.com/app. Using the tool means accepting them. How your files are handled is a separate question, answered in the privacy policy.

The project was built as part of the University of Washington Advancing Accessibility for Engineering Education initiative, but it is not an official University of Washington service. It is a student-led open-source project, run on a project-owned AWS account. UW does not operate it, support it, or answer for it.

It costs nothing to use. There is no paid tier, no invoice and no contract behind it.

The tool is offered as is

A file the tool has fixed is not guaranteed to meet WCAG, Section 508, the ADA, or any other standard or law. It is not an audit, a certification, or a substitute for one. It fixes a specific set of common problems — reading order, missing alt text, missing language, small text — and it misses things outside that set.

Every run produces a report saying what was changed and what still needs a person. Read it. The alt text in particular is written by a language model from the picture and the words around it: it is a first draft that is often right and sometimes wrong, and it needs someone who knows the material to check it before the deck goes to students. You are responsible for what you hand out, not us.

There is no promise that the tool is available, that it will finish your file, or that it will not get something wrong. It is a research project under active development, and parts of it change often. Keep your original file.

What you may upload

  • Only material you have the right to share. If the deck isn't yours, make sure you are allowed to send a copy of it to a third-party service before you do.
  • No student records. Don't upload anything containing grades, student names attached to work, disability or accommodation records, or anything else protected by FERPA. The tool is for course material — lecture slides and handouts.
  • Nothing else confidential. No personal data about other people, no health or financial records, no unpublished research under embargo, nothing under NDA.
  • Supported files only. PowerPoint files, and PDFs where the server you are using accepts them. Each file must be under 50 MB, and everything sent at once under 250 MB.

Your files stay yours. Uploading gives us no claim over your material, and no right to use it for anything beyond running the pipeline you asked for.

Fair use of the service

The service runs on a small budget. These rules exist so it stays up for everyone.

  • Don't script bulk uploads or otherwise drive it automatically. If you have a large batch to get through, the tool also runs as a command-line script on your own machine — see the repository.
  • Don't try to reach submissions that aren't yours, guess at identifiers, or work around the limits on who can download what.
  • Don't probe, overload or attempt to break the service, and don't use it to process anything illegal.
  • Don't resell it or present it as your own service.

Uploads may be rate-limited or blocked if any of this becomes a problem. Nothing here is meant to stop ordinary use by a faculty member with a term's worth of lectures to fix.

Availability and changes to the tool

Features may change, and the hosted service may be paused or shut down, with or without notice. This is a student project with a funding horizon, not a product with a support contract. If it does shut down, the code stays public so the tool can still be run locally.

Your files are deleted on a timer in any case — uploads after 7 days, results after 30. The privacy policy has the detail.

That includes a file the tool damaged or failed to process, work lost because a file expired or the service was unavailable, alt text that turned out to be wrong, and any consequence of relying on a fixed file being accessible or compliant when it was not.

This is a free tool given away by a university project. It comes with no warranty of any kind.

Changes to these terms
