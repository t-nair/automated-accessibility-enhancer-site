Privacy Policy

We collect as little as we can, your lecture files are deleted on a timer whether you ask or not, and nothing here is sold, shared for advertising or used to train anybody's model.

Last updated Nov 19, 2026

What this covers

This policy covers this website and the tool it links to at accessibilityenhancer.com/app, which fixes reading order and writes missing alt text in PowerPoint files.

The project was built as part of the University of Washington Advancing Accessibility for Engineering Education initiative, but it is not an official University of Washington service. It is a student-led open-source project, run on a project-owned AWS account, and UW does not operate it or hold the files you send it.

The code is public, so anything written here can be checked against it: the tool and this site.

What we collect and why

The guiding rule is to collect only what the tool needs to do the job you asked it to do.

The files you upload

When you send a presentation, we store the file you uploaded so the pipeline can work on it. Nothing else is done with it: it is not read by a person, not used as training data, and not shared with anyone outside the processors listed below.

Please don't upload files containing student records, grades or anything else protected by FERPA, or any other confidential or personal information. The tool is for course material. Only upload material you have the right to share.

What the tool writes back

The fixed copy of your file and the report of every change made to it are stored so you can download them. The report includes the alt text written for each image.

Details about each submission

Alongside the files we store a short record of the submission: a random submission id, the original file name, the language you chose for the deck, when it was submitted, its status, how many times it was retried, and the text of any error. This is what draws your status page.

A session cookie

The site sets one cookie, which is how your list of files is kept separate from everyone else's. It holds a random id generated for your browser and a token that protects forms against cross-site request forgery. It is signed so it cannot be edited, and it expires after 30 days. It is not an advertising or tracking cookie, and it is not shared with anyone.

Because that id lives only in the cookie, clearing your cookies loses your list of files. The files themselves stay until their timer runs out.

Server logs

The website and the pipeline write ordinary operational logs: the time, what was requested, submission ids, uploaded file names and sizes, and any errors. They are what makes it possible to work out why a deck failed. They do not contain the contents of your slides.

Your account, once sign-in is switched on

Signing in is not live yet; today the cookie above is the only identity the site has. When it is switched on, accounts will be held in Amazon Cognito, which will store your email address, whether it has been verified, and your name if you choose to sign in with Google. Cognito handles the password itself, so we never see it. Verification and password-reset email is sent through Amazon SES. Accounts are keyed by the identifier Cognito issues, never by email address.

Who else processes it

Four companies touch your files or your details, each for one job.

  • Amazon Web Services, in the us-east-1 region, runs all of it: S3 holds the uploads and the results, DynamoDB holds the submission records, Lambda runs the pipeline, ECS runs the website, and Cognito and SES will handle accounts and their email. Files are encrypted at rest and only reachable over HTTPS.
  • Claude on Amazon Bedrock writes the alt text. Each picture that has no alt text is sent to the model on its own, scaled down, together with up to 400 characters of the text on the same slide so the description comes out right. Nothing else from the deck is sent, and no account details are sent. AWS states that content processed by Bedrock is encrypted and stored at rest in the region where Bedrock is used, that inputs and outputs are not shared with the model provider, and that they are not used to train AWS or third-party models; AWS may retain content for safety and abuse prevention. The request uses a US cross-region inference profile, so it may be processed in any US region rather than only us-east-1.
  • Cloudflare serves this site and proxies the tool at /app, so every request and every upload passes through it on the way to AWS.
  • Google, only if you choose to sign in with Google once sign-in is switched on, and only for your email address and name.

How long it's kept

Deletion is automatic. Nobody has to remember to do it, and you do not have to ask.

WhatHow long
The file you uploaded7 days
The fixed file and its report30 days
The submission record30 days
Server logs30 days
The session cookie30 days
Your account, once accounts existUntil you delete it

Two things outlive those numbers slightly, both so that a mistake can be undone:

  • Deleted files can be restored for 3 more days. The buckets keep previous versions, so a file that is deleted or overwritten is recoverable for three days afterwards and is then removed for good. The longest anything can survive is its number above plus those three days.
  • The submission table keeps continuous backups for up to 35 days. That covers the records described above, not the files themselves.

What we don't do

  • No selling or sharing. Your files and your details are never sold, rented or handed to anyone for their own purposes.
  • No advertising, and no analytics trackers. This site loads no third-party scripts, no fonts from anyone else's server, no pixels and no analytics of any kind. The one cookie it sets is described above.
  • No training on your material. Your slides are not used to train or fine-tune any model, ours or anybody else's.
  • No reading your decks. Nobody on the project opens the files you upload. The only exception would be a specific problem you ask us to look into, with your permission.

Deleting things sooner

  • A submission. Open it from your status page and use Delete. That removes the upload, the fixed file, the report and the record, ahead of the timer. A file still being processed has to finish first.
  • Everything in your browser. Clearing cookies detaches you from your list. The files are then unreachable and expire on their own schedule.
  • Your account, once accounts exist. Ask at the address below and it will be deleted, along with the submissions attached to it.

You can also ask what is held about you, ask for it to be corrected, or ask for it to be deleted, by writing to the address below.

How it's kept secure

  • Everything travels over HTTPS, and the storage buckets refuse plain HTTP outright.
  • Files are encrypted at rest in S3, and the submission table is encrypted too.
  • The buckets are closed to the public. Downloads use links that are signed for your submission and expire after five minutes.
  • A submission can only be opened, downloaded or deleted by the browser or account that sent it.
  • The deploy pipeline is explicitly denied permission to read the uploaded or processed files.

No system is perfect, and this one is a small open-source project rather than a hardened commercial service. That is part of why the files are deleted quickly: the less there is, the less there is to lose.

Where it's stored

In the United States. If you are somewhere else and you use the tool, you are sending your files to the US to be processed there.

Changes to this policy

The date at the top changes whenever this page does, and the edit itself is in the commit history of this file, so you can see exactly what changed and when. Anything significant will also be noted in the updates on the front page.
